**What to write down for each game**
1. Seconds from spawn until you first did something fun.
2. Seconds one job takes from start to reward: one wash, one room or one customer.
3. The minute it first felt finished or dull.
4. Whether what you got was ever a surprise.
5. What would bring you back tomorrow, if anything.
6. Whether other players mattered.
7. What happens when a customer waits too long and what the staff take over.

**The team also asked for**
- Screens: A screenshot of the phone screen during a job and of the card after a job helps the most.
- Store: The icon or picture that made you want to tap, if any.

**Played and saved**
- Save a Horse: Wash & Collect, the horse game: Played November 24. Your verdict: another Steal an Egg copy with nothing really to do with cleaning, where waiting for the horse to be clean is the same as waiting for an egg to hatch. What we take from it: in ours the wash is always done by hand and a timer never stands in for it. Waits belong to builds and upgrades only. From your five pictures: one clear step line at the top with a short line under it and arrows on the floor work well. Its tutorial opens a shop with Robux prices beside the coin prices, which ours never does in the first session.
- Clean Everything: Played November 24. Your summary: you choose a map, load into it with 1 to 4 players and pick up scattered items and place them where they belong until the place is clean. So it is a sorting game with friends, not a wash game. What we take, from your eight pictures: it gives players a lot to chase besides the cleaning, with nine maps and three more shown as question marks, two bars that count every object and shelf, and a glowing target that shows where the thing in your hand goes. That fits why it kept its players. What we avoid: two loading screens before you play, a Robux offer on the screen from the first second, a reward for a like and a group join, helpers rented by the minute for Robux and a watch an ad button. One bakery holds 1,040 objects, which is a long job for one sitting.
- Restaurant Tycoon 3: Played November 24. Your finding: they do not use friends as waiters or customers, and we can take some ideas from them. Ideas we take, from your 20 pictures: one goal bar at the top with a count, such as Cook 5 dishes, 0 of 5. Work that is one short swipe with a bar that fills and a Cancel button. A trail from you to the customer with a plain line that says what to do. A line that says a VIP customer just came in, with a Show Me button. An upgrades window with a tab for what you can buy now and a tab for what is locked, where each locked one says what it requires, so the long road is in view. Workers with a name, a job, a hire price and a price to level up. Not taken: three setup screens before the first customer, a starter pack with a countdown on the screen in the first minute and a premium worker in the hire list.
- My Pet Salon!: You liked the different kinds of step, the praise words with a growing number, the star card with coins and experience after each pet, tool upgrades, the index, rare customers with a lasting reward and hired helpers. You disliked the cutscene with no skip, the same swipe three times, 16 steps in one job, the pet turning by itself and only one customer at a time. First fun thing at about 75 seconds.
- Capybara Onsen: Not a spa game: step on pads, buy and merge. Useful all the same: a short tutorial with a free first buy, a friend bonus percent on screen from the start and a request for a favourite at about minute 4.
