# Retention mechanics: evidence for D1 and D7, and fairness for children (read 2026-11-24)

Tags: [official] [data] [research] [opinion]. All pages were read on 2026-11-24 through a summarizing fetch tool, so quotes are as the tool returned them; re-read key pages before relying on them. DevForum threads redirected to a maintenance page and could not be read (including the daily-rewards retention thread 3556728 and the notification rate-limits thread). Not repeated here: the contents of the two earlier notes.

Headline: no controlled test or published Roblox number was found that isolates any of these mechanics for D1 or D7. The strongest evidence is design writing plus platform and regulator rules. The only hard numbers are session-length correlations and unrelated experiments.

## 1. Evidence per mechanic

### Takeaway
No source gives a measured D1 or D7 lift for offline earning, timers, daily rewards, streaks, collections, shop rotation, events or clans in a Roblox or young-audience setting. Evidence is mostly practitioner opinion; the few numbers are old, non-Roblox, or about a different thing. Confidence is highest for "frequent small reasons to return are standard" and lowest for "offline timers raise D1".

### Cited findings

Offline or idle earning, wait timers
- [data] GameAnalytics Roblox report 2025 (page "Last updated Aug 24, 2026", data to mid-2025): qualitative only. It credits "offline/idle progression and time-gated rewards" with the 2025 rise in Core User return frequency, but "the report doesn't isolate their effect on retention." Its closest numbers are median D1/D7 by average session length (0-3 min 4.31%/0.41%; 25+ min 10.76%/1.82%), a correlation, not a feature effect. [GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/cn/reports/2025-roblox-report)
- [opinion] GameAnalytics idle-game guide (Mar 17, 2025): "Timed in-app economies raise retention. Players return daily to collect currency..." No figure supports it. Idle benchmark in the same article: D1 45.55% for the top 10% of idle games, 39.40% for the top 25% (mobile idle, not Roblox, not children). [GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/how-to-keep-players-engaged-and-coming-back-to-your-idle-game)
- [opinion] Naavik, Jul 6, 2025: Grow a Garden uses "idle-inspired timers and resource collection"; top Roblox hits' "systems for retention and monetization are lifted from mobile free-to-play"; "battle passes, limited-time events, and collection economies" are standard. No numbers. [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/predicting-the-next-big-hits-on-roblox/)
- [opinion] Game Developer, Jun 13, 2016 (Grosso): appointment mechanics work through loss aversion. Examples: FarmVille tomatoes "need to be watered in the next 2 hours"; Clash Royale chests open after 3, 4, 8 or 24 hours. The article does not warn that waits are too long and does not mention Clash of Clans or Hay Day. [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-big-games-use-appointment-mechanics-to-get-gamers-keep-coming-back)
- [opinion] Pocket Camp review, Dec 7, 2017: waiting is the "key friction point to spend"; calls the game "very light in terms of long-term retention features." Author's prediction, not data. [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/animal-crossing-pocket-camp-can-an-old-dog-learn-new-tricks-)

Daily login rewards and streaks
- [opinion] Game Developer, Jun 13, 2016 (Grosso): no retention numbers; streaks "can feel punishing when broken"; Warframe resets to the lowest tier after one missed day and players "interpret as a punishment"; mitigations are cumulative rewards that do not reset and strong early rewards after a reset. Cookie Run and Warframe tier examples. [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-science-craft-of-designing-daily-rewards-and-why-ftp-games-need-them)
- [opinion] RoWatcher, Jun 5, 2026: daily bonuses "churn accelerator disguised as a retention mechanic." The page "presents no original or quantified data." Treat as opinion only. [RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/news/reward-schedules-in-roblox-why-daily-login-bonuses-backfire)
- [research] Jiao, Tang, Wang, Production and Operations Management 2022 (UCLA Anderson summary, Jun 15, 2022): over 15,000 players of a China-based PvP mobile game over three years: both winning and losing streaks were linked to "a significant drop in playing time the following week" (no percentage). This is about play streaks, not login streaks, and adults. [UCLA Anderson](https://www.anderson-review.ucla.edu/what-drives-engagement-in-116-billion-mobile-gaming-industry/)

Collections and novelty
- Same study: acquiring a new character gave "nearly 33% increase in playing time in the following week" (same 15,000-player population, a novelty effect, not an index). Clan members played about 4% more and made nearly 3% more purchases than non-members (correlation; selection effects likely).
- Searches for collection-index retention data returned only blogs; none had data. Not found.

Unfinished goals at exit, rotating shops, events
- No data found for any of the three. Roblox's onboarding doc advises "short-, mid- and long-term goals in highly visible places" (earlier note; undated).

Notifications
- [data, old] Urban Airship Good Push Index via PocketGamer.biz, Dec 11, 2013: first-month retention 55% for opt-in users vs 29% for opt-out (survey of 2,400 apps and 500 million notifications; user counts not given). Self-selection applies. [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/chart-of-the-week-why-getting-players-to-opt-in-to-game-push-notifications-matters/)
- [data, vendor] Mistplay summarising Airship 2023 (665 billion notifications, 2.5 billion users): games that "send push notifications within the first 90 days have three times the retention rate". Vendor claim, correlation, mobile, adults. [Mistplay](https://business.mistplay.com/resources/mobile-game-retention-benchmarks)
- Roblox opt-in rates: none published (see section 4).

Related non-feature data
- [research] Ascarza, Netzer, Runge, Dec 2023: randomized test, about 330,000 eligible players of a puzzle game; easier boards lifted next-day retention by 0.027 from a control of about 0.295, and 7-day by 0.025. Shows that a gentler game raised retention; it is not a timer or reward test. [Columbia](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/citation_file_upload/ascarza_netzer_runge_2023-12-04.pdf)
- [data, old] deltaDNA via Game Developer, Apr 18, 2016: games with a first session over the 9-minute mean averaged 31% D1 vs about 20% below it (275 games, Oct 2015 to Feb 2016); author warns causation may run the other way. [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/how-first-session-length-impacts-game-performance)

### Inferences (mine)
- Because Roblox's own 2026 signals are play days in Day 1, Day 2-7 and Day 8-28 (earlier note), any mechanic that gives a reason for one more distinct day matters more than one that lengthens a session.
- Better first-session play (longer, smoother sessions) has more support in the numbers than any return hook.

### Gaps
No A/B or cohort result for any mechanic on Roblox; no kids-specific data; no Roblox genre benchmark.

## 2. Timer lengths

### Takeaway
Mobile builder and farming games start with timers of seconds to a couple of minutes and keep early waits short. Roblox farming games with offline growth use seconds for first crops and hours for late ones. I found no data on the point where a wait pushes players away; only design opinion.

### Cited findings
- [fan guide] Hay Day: wheat "takes only 2 minutes to reach harvest"; chickens lay eggs every 20 minutes if fed. Overnight timers not given on that page. Date not shown. [AppGamer](https://www.appgamer.com/hay-day/strategy-guide/first-five-hours)
- [fan guide] Clash of Clans: the first session lasts "between 30 minutes and one hour" before free play; Builder's Hut costs Gems; new players get a shield for "the first three" days. Upgrade times not on the page. [AppGamer](https://www.appgamer.com/clash-of-clans/strategy-guide/first-five-hours)
- [fan guide] Grow a Garden (TechWiser, Jan 2, 2026): Carrot 2 seconds; Strawberry 8 seconds then 4 seconds per fruit; Spring Onion 22 seconds; Corn 6 min 40 s; Evo Beetroot 18 min 40 s; top common crop 2 h 33 min; Untold Bell 10 h 21 min 44 s. Tier lengthens with rarity. [TechWiser](https://techwiser.com/grow-a-garden-crops-growth-time-harvest-cycle/)
- [opinion] Game Developer 2016: Clash Royale chests 3, 4, 8, 24 hours; FarmVille crops 6 to 7.5 hours, matched to when a player is free.
- Cookie Run Kingdom, Animal Crossing Pocket Camp first timers: not found. Pocket Camp: only "crafting takes time" and a one-slot start (2017 opinion).
- [opinion] PocketGamer.biz energy-mechanics piece (Jul 8, 2014): no churn data; "millions ... are happy to come back every day".

### Inferences (mine)
- The consistent pattern is seconds, then minutes, then hours as the player progresses, with the first overnight wait arriving after the player is invested. A first-session 8-hour build conflicts with that pattern and with Roblox's own advice to give a "deliberately designed moment of joy" in the first session (earlier note).
- A seconds-long timer is not an appointment; it is a feedback beat.

### Gaps
No source for when a wait turns from pull to push; no first overnight timer figure for any named game; no kids data.

## 3. Rotating and restocking shops

### Takeaway
Grow a Garden's shop restocks every five minutes on a shared clock, so players check in often and can return to see what appeared. No data on retention effect was found. Critics call the wider monetization close to gambling; a restock shop for children should avoid paid rerolls and scarcity pressure.

### Cited findings
- [dev, DevForum] A developer reverse-engineering post says items "will restock after 5 minutes", seeded from the shared server clock so every server shows the same stock (the author says it is "one of the many ways", not confirmed). [DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/reverse-engineering-on-how-the-stock-system-in-grow-a-garden-works/3767345)
- [fan guide, earlier note] Rare seeds average over a day of expected waiting (Dragon's Breath about 1d 17h 40m, article about Grow a Garden 2).
- [opinion] Jacob Navok, GamesRadar, Jul 18, 2025: calls Grow a Garden "somewhat evil", asks whether players pay to speed growth or steal; says its edge is close to gambling. [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/former-square-enix-exec-says-viral-roblox-hit-grow-a-garden-is-somewhat-evil-do-you-wanna-pay-to-grow-this-faster-do-you-wanna-pay-to-steal-this-from-someones-garden)
- [opinion/petition] A change.org petition, created Jul 28, 2025 (89 verified signatures): a daily class stock system in 99 Nights in the Forest, paid rerolls, forces players to wait "a day to several days". Tiny sample. [change.org](https://www.change.org/p/remove-the-stocks-system-in-roblox-s-99-nights-in-a-forest)
- [research] University of Sydney, Mar 24, 2025: interviews with 22 children aged 7-14 (18 played Roblox); children and parents called deceptive spending features "scams," "cash grabs" and "child gambling"; Grow a Garden was not named, Adopt Me, Blox Fruits, Pet Simulator 99 and Royal High were. About 42% of players under 13 (the study's figure). [Sydney](https://sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2025/03/24/literally-just-child-gambling-study-urges-swift-regulation-of-roblox.html)

### Inferences (mine)
- Why it holds players: a short fixed clock gives a goal inside any session and a reason to stay or come back; the random roll adds a variable reward. This is my reading; no source tested it.
- Fairness: free, fixed-schedule, no-loss restocks with a visible next-restock time look fine. Paid rerolls, hidden odds and "gone forever" rares look grey to bad.

### Gaps
No retention or session data on restock shops; no published odds for Grow a Garden.

## 4. Roblox experience notifications (page last_updated 2026-11-23T04:58:48Z)

### Takeaway
Notifications are opt-in and aimed at 13+. Under-13s can receive only update notifications. Dark patterns are barred in the content. No opt-in rates are published.

### Cited findings [official] ([Creator Docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/promotion/experience-notifications.md))
- Recipients: "opted-in users age 13+"; under-13s "are only able to receive notification of experience updates".
- Prompt: displayed to users 13+; "The prompt's text is not customizable and is standardized across all experiences." Not shown if the user "Is under the age of 13" or "Has already seen the permission prompt for your experience in the past 30 days."
- Limits: "each user can receive one notification per day from a given experience"; also "Recipient's aggregate daily throttle limit has been reached." The aggregate number is not stated on this page.
- Banned: "Notification content is not permitted to incorporate dark patterns"; disguised ads; "Notifications that pressure users into clicking, subscribing, consenting, or purchasing" (listed as Time Pressured Actions); bait-and-switch ("falsely tell users that they'll receive something for free when it's not"); tricking users into purchases. Also "Games should not require users to turn on notifications in order to participate or advance in gameplay."
- Opt-in rates: none published. The page only defines the "Opted-in Users" metric.
- Earlier note adds: 100+ visits, 99 character string.

### Inferences (mine)
Under-13 players cannot opt in to return reminders, so notifications cannot be the core return mechanic for a young audience. Whether a 13+ minority exists in the cat spa audience is unknown.

### Gaps
Aggregate daily throttle value, opt-in rates, effect on D1 or D7: not found.

## 5. Children, regulators and platform rules

### Takeaway
No regulator was found that bans login rewards, streaks or timers by name. Rules target pressure, manipulation, unclear pricing and random paid items. Missed-day loss, countdowns and urgency tied to spending are the clearest risks.

### Cited findings
- [official] UK Age Appropriate Design Code, detrimental use of data: strategies to extend engagement, "can include mechanisms such as reward loops, continuous scrolling, notifications and auto-play features"; targets "features which use personal data to exploit human susceptibility to reward, anticipatory and pleasure seeking behaviours." The page does not mention streaks. [ICO standard 5](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/5-detrimental-use-of-data/). Note the wording is about use of personal data.
- [official] ICO games guidance (Feb 2023): players should disengage "without feeling pressurised to continue playing or becoming fearful of missing out"; checkpoints and prompts to take breaks. [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2023/02/new-guidance-to-industry-issued-for-game-developers-on-protecting-children)
- [official] ICO nudge page: nudges toward wellbeing such as breaks are fine; "Present options in ways that encourage conscious decision making". [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/13-nudge-techniques/)
- [official] EU CPC key principles on in-game currencies adopted 21 March 2025: "respecting consumer vulnerabilities, in particular when it comes to children". Loot boxes: games "must not include direct exhortations to children to buy" add-ons (Commission letter, 5 Jul 2023). [EC](https://commission.europa.eu/live-work-travel-eu/consumer-rights-and-complaints/enforcement-consumer-protection/coordinated-actions/social-media-online-games-and-search-engines_en)
- [research/consumer body] vzbv, Mar 11, 2025 (testing Jul-Sep 2024): lists "attach countdowns to certain offers to put users under pressure", a "Play daily to get rewards!" pop-up and "incentives for daily logins" among manipulative designs. Does not name streaks. Adopt Me was cited for a countdown purchase icon. [vzbv](https://www.vzbv.de/sites/default/files/2025-04/25-03-11_Paper_vzbv_Manipulative_design_unfair_practices_online_games.pdf)
- [official] FTC, Jan 17, 2025 (Genshin Impact): in-game system "particularly confusing to children and teens"; odds misleading; loot boxes sold with virtual currency need a dollar option. About random paid items and COPPA, not timers. [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/01/level-tips-businesses-ftcs-settlement-genshin-impact-developer-hoyoverse)
- [official] Roblox Community Standards: bans "misleading content or schemes"; paid random items need odds disclosed "before the moment of purchase", no outcome where the user loses payment; ads not personalized under 18. No explicit pressure-tactic rule on the page; the notification page has one. [Roblox](https://about.roblox.com/community-standards)

### Inferences (mine): traffic-light
- Clearly fine: reward for coming back with no loss when skipped (growth waiting, tip jar filling with a clear cap, collection that never expires); short timers in seconds to minutes; visible goals; a free daily gift that does not reset.
- Grey: streaks with a gentle catch-up; offline timers of hours; fixed-clock restocks (free); limited-time events with an obvious return date; daily login pop-ups.
- Avoid for under-13s: streak loss or pet death; countdown on a purchase; Robux to skip or reroll in a free-gift context; paid random items without odds; false urgency; notifications that pressure; things that require notifications.

### Gaps
Roblox's pressure policy for in-experience design (not notifications) was not found. AADC language on streaks, and EU/FTC positions on login rewards for under-13s specifically, not found. A lawyer should review.

## 6. Ranked list for a young-audience Roblox cat spa

1. Short, free, session-length timers (seconds to minutes) that finish while playing, plus a stay-a-bit reason. Evidence: some. Sessions of 25+ minutes show D1 10.76% vs 4.31% for 0-3 minutes (GameAnalytics, correlation), and Hay Day/Grow a Garden start with seconds and minutes. Cat spa: towel dries in 20 seconds, fur-fluff treatment in 2 minutes, cat naps in 5.
2. Collection index with a visible next unfinished page. Evidence: thin to some (33% playtime rise after acquiring a new character in one study; Naavik says collection economies are standard). Cat spa: breed and fur-pattern stamps with 3 empty slots shown at exit; never expiring.
3. Free daily gift with no streak loss (cumulative or no-reset coin track). Evidence: thin to some. Opinion sources favor non-resetting rewards; no D1 data. Cat spa: one gift cat visit per day; calendar of coins that never resets. Adopt Me's coins do not need to be consecutive (earlier note).
4. Capped away-earnings (tip jar) of modest length, e.g. an hour or two, collected on return. Evidence: thin to some (GameAnalytics credits time-gated rewards with higher return frequency, unmeasured). Cat spa: tip jar fills while away, cap shown, welcome-back animation; save the 8-hour build until after the first return.
5. Weekly or seasonal events with a fixed, visible schedule and a free path. Evidence: some for long-lived Roblox games (Grow a Garden weekly updates, DTI events, earlier note), no numbers. Cat spa: weekly guest-cat theme; free reward; no countdown on purchases. Note update notifications are the only kind under-13s can get.

Not ranked: notifications (closed to under 13), clans (social risk, 13+ chat limits), paid skip timers (grey to avoid), rotating shop with paid reroll (avoid).

### Gaps
The biggest gap is a D1 or D7 lift for any item above. A cheap fix is to A/B the studio's own versions with Roblox Experiments, as the onboarding doc suggests.
