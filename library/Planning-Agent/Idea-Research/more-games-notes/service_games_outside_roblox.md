## 1. Customers: queue, choice, patience, leaving

### Takeaway
The classic hits all make waiting visible and let the player choose who to serve next. The cost of a miss differs a lot. Diner Dash and Cooking Fever make a leaving customer cost points or the level. The softer cat and animal idle games have no leave penalty in the sources read. Choosing between waiting customers is the core "dilemma" of the genre, and players often fixate on one customer while others sit.

### Cited findings
- Diner Dash: each customer has a row of hearts that drains with waiting; talking to them or serving drinks restores hearts; a customer who loses all hearts leaves and costs points; patience and tip habits vary by customer type. [data, Wikipedia, secondary, read 2026-11-24]. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diner_Dash
- Diner Dash: falling short of a level's money target costs one star, and the game ends when all stars are lost. [data, same page]
- Treanor and Nelson, "Order-Fulfillment Games", FDG 2019: each customer is "effectively a countdown timer"; players must choose which timer to serve because moving costs time; they call this a dilemma; a common bad habit is fixating on one customer. Players feel responsible for failures; common feelings are anxiety, decisiveness, frustration and a juggling "zany" feel. [research] http://www.kmjn.org/publications/OrderFulfillment_FDG19.pdf (fetch summary)
- Sally's Spa: blue hearts show patience, red hearts show happiness and tips. Customers differ in patience and tip size, and high tippers tend to be the least patient, so serving them first pays most. Tea, a patience candle and some chairs raise patience. [opinion/guide, Gamezebo walkthrough, 2008, edited 2023] https://www.gamezebo.com/walkthroughs/sallys-spa-tips-walkthrough/
- Cooking Craze: impatient customers flash nasty looks and "turn into animals" if kept waiting; in some levels waiting decides win or loss. [opinion, VICE, 2018-01-16] https://www.vice.com/en/article/cooking-craze-freemium-mobile-game/
- Papa's Pizzeria: the player must stop and take each order, orders do not appear on their own; the wait was meant to make multitasking harder; goal was "not unbearably frustrating". [developer statement, Flipline post-mortem, 2014-05-01] https://www.flipline.com/blog/?p=3887
- Papa's series: no wait timers described on the page read; accurate and quick orders earn tips and stars, and stars promote regulars to Bronze, Silver, Gold for more tips. [data, Wikipedia]
- Good Pizza Great Pizza: over 100 customers with personal looks and orders; players may accept a customer who pays less than the full order; patience and timers are not mentioned on the page read. [data, Wikipedia]
- Furista's Cat Cafe: customers arrive at the top and you match each to a cat of the right personality, then they cuddle and leave heart notes; the reviewer praises the "near-constant flow of customers" and low stakes. [opinion, Pocket Gamer review, 2018-07-26, 4.0] https://www.pocketgamer.com/furistas-cat-cafe/review
- Pokemon Cafe ReMix: visitors come in a random queue; serve them until their Heart meter fills and they join staff. Levels are not timed but move-limited. [data, Smogon, 2022-04-08] https://www.smogon.com/articles/spinoff-spotlight-cafe
- Animal Restaurant: no customer timer or patience mechanic stated; customer types (regular, special, performers) are collected in a journal. Tapping orders speeds turnover. [data, PocketGamer.biz/AppQuantum, 2023-05-30] https://www.pocketgamer.biz/animal-restaurant-how-to-earn-2000000-in-iaps-with-a-cute-mobile-game

### Inferences (mine)
- Patience is the one lever that creates the "who next" decision. Without it, order does not matter and a queue is just a line.
- Sally's Spa shows a cheap way to make the choice interesting without harsh loss: customer types differ (tip size versus patience), so the best pick is not always the nearest.

### Gaps
- No source gave exact patience duration in seconds for any game.
- Cooking Fever patience mechanics: Wikipedia page had none; Common Sense says customers become "visibly annoyed" when slow (below).

## 2. Helpers, staff and automation

### Takeaway
Helpers arrive after the player has learned the hands-on task, and they take the boring or repeated step, not the whole job. In the Sally's Spa style the player stays hands-on for the fun step. In the idle cat and animal games, staff take over almost everything and the player taps.

### Cited findings
- Sally's Spa: four assistants (tea maker, massage therapist, sauna assistant, bath assistant). Examples: the tea maker brews cups automatically; the massage therapist does massages so you only apply hot stones; the sauna assistant turns the sauna on. Assistants can attend Charm School from the 5th location for extra heart boosts. Assistants cost real money in the mobile version ($2.99 to $4.99, earlier notes). [opinion/guide plus store data] Gamezebo link above.
- Two Point Hospital: you hire doctors, nurses, janitors, assistants; staff have personality and may fight or chat when left alone; designers say you must "pull them apart and put them back to work". [developer statement, PCGamesN interview, undated] https://www.pcgamesn.com/two-point-hospital/two-point-hospital-developer-interview
- Papa's: custom workers only appear in later sequels. [developer statement, Flipline, 2014]
- Pokemon Cafe ReMix: recruited visitors become staff with a skill and food specialty; adding staff unlocks cafe expansions. [data, Smogon]
- Eatventure and Cat Snack Bar: staff and upgraded stations run even offline; an "invite helper" gives 5 minutes of boost every 30 minutes (Eatventure); special worker cats are sold as IAP in Cat Snack Bar; the player serves directly in Cat Snack Bar. [opinion/analysis, PocketGamer.biz, AppQuantum, 2023-10-20] https://www.pocketgamer.biz/feature/82671/eatventure-vs-cat-snack-bar-an-analysis-of-both-games/
- Animal Restaurant: staff hired with the same coin currency, but collection still needs player taps (no auto-collect). [data, PocketGamer.biz 2023]
- Furista's Cat Cafe: your cats level up to serve higher-level customers who give more hearts. [opinion, review]

### Inferences (mine)
- A slower helper fits Sally's Spa well: helper does one step (fill the tub) while the player does the hands-on step.
- Selling helpers for real money is common in the sources (Sally's Spa assistants, Cat Snack Bar cats). That is a pay-wall risk for a young audience.

### Gaps
- No source states helper speed relative to the player.

## 3. Scoring per job

### Takeaway
Tips are the common unit. Better and faster service earns more. Stars grade the whole day, not the job.

### Cited findings
- Diner Dash: repeating the same action builds a chain bonus; a different action breaks it. Each level has a point target and an "expert" target. [data, Wikipedia] The designer of a sequel calls the chain "satisfying" and says shrunken tap targets caused misclicks that broke it, annoying casual players. [developer statement, Baggatta, Game Developer, 2010-06-29] https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/finding-the-game-in-a-sequel-to-a-sequel-to-a-sequel
- Sally's Spa: full smile pays the maximum and adds a heart; half smile pays less; a frown costs a heart. A tip candle boosts the tip when ringing up. Queueing up to nine tasks adds no money but helps organise. Customers who are ready should be rung up quickly. [guide, Gamezebo]
- Papa's: each task graded on performance (cut angle, topping placement); first scoring was tips only, and players wanted something to spend them on, so later games added a points tally for ranks plus a shop. A food critic orders something new and gives a 3-day tip boost. [developer statement and data] Flipline link; Wikipedia.
- Two Point Hospital: one star unlocks the next hospital, more stars are optional extras. Designer Carr: "sweet spot is somewhere around two stars", three is "a bit full-on". [developer statement, PCGamesN]
- Overcooked: assist mode can change points per completed order. [data, GameRant, undated]

### Inferences (mine)
- A calm version: stars are earned by finishing, with extra stars for fast or careful work. No star is lost for a customer who left.

### Gaps
- No end-of-day breakdown screens were documented in any source read.

## 4. What brings players back

### Takeaway
Makers name three things: a steady flow of new places or content, a base the player decorates, and collecting. Sources are mostly makers' own words.

### Cited findings
- Cooking Fever: producer says constant updates are the key; each new restaurant adds mechanics and cuisines and brings loyal players back; a garden gives something to do while away and free gems; 42 restaurants, over 450M downloads at July 2024. [developer statement and data, PocketGamer.biz, 2024-07-10] https://www.pocketgamer.biz/celebrating-10-years-of-cooking-fever-and-nordcurrents-sustained-success
- Good Pizza Great Pizza: seasonal events (a 2021 summer garden event became permanent), decor themes, 20+ toppings and over a million possible orders; players express themselves through the shop. [developer statement, Game Developer, 2024-06-05] https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/good-pizza-great-pizza-would-not-exist-if-not-for-costco
- Papa's: customer files with name, look and favourite order; players complained when favourites were missing in later games. [developer statement, 2014]
- Animal Restaurant: collections (customers, mementos, letters), daily login packs over a month, limited-time events, prestige to open more restaurants while keeping old ones. [analyst, AppQuantum, 2023]
- Sally's Spa: new locations at level targets. [store data, earlier notes]
- Pokemon Cafe ReMix: about 2,000 levels, about 75 new levels every two weeks at first, team events of up to 30, recruiting and outfits. [data, Smogon, 2022]

### Inferences (mine)
- Regular customers with names and favourite things (Papa's, GPGP) suit cats well: a returning cat is a reason to come back.

### Gaps
- No D1 or D7 retention numbers for any of these games, except the figures in section 5.

## 6. Criticism

- Cooking Fever: Common Sense rates it 10+; customers "visibly annoyed" when you are slow; "some kids may get frustrated"; parents say it feels impossible to advance without spending and complain about lost progress [parent opinion, Common Sense, updated 2025-10-09]. https://www.commonsensemedia.org/app-reviews/cooking-fever
- Cooking Craze: some levels near-impossible without paying for 30 more seconds; reviewer spent $100.47 [opinion, VICE, 2018].
- Sally's Spa: difficulty spikes, upgrade and assistant cost, travel timers (earlier notes).
- Pokemon Cafe ReMix: energy of 5 with a 15 minute refill pushes waiting or paying; grind to recruit; gacha-like delivery costs [opinion, Smogon, 2022].
- Diner Dash sequel: added features made testers confused or angry; a Zoom feature hurt usability and was dropped [developer statement, 2010].
- Overcooked 2: chaotic interface and visibility in busy moments [opinion, Wikipedia summary of reviews].
- Two Point Hospital: repetitive, rebuilding from scratch each hospital [opinion, Wikipedia].
- Pizza Shop Mania: Common Sense 9+, needs quick thinking and fine motor skill, "best for slightly older children" [opinion, Common Sense]. Diner Dash is also 9+ there.

## 7. Time pressure and children

### Takeaway
I found no study on patience meters or fail states for ages 6 to 12. What exists is age ratings, developer advice from Overcooked and Two Point, and one cozy-game essay. Evidence is thin.

### Cited findings
- Common Sense age ratings: Diner Dash 9+, Pizza Shop Mania 9+, Cooking Fever 10+ (kids say 8+). Text for Diner Dash says quick thinking and fine motor skills needed. [opinion]
- Overcooked: team started with lives, switched to a timer because lives "bogged down" players; panic was deliberate. [developer statement, MCV Develop post-mortem, 2017-06-26] https://mcvuk.com/development-news/the-develop-post-mortem-overcooked/. Later All You Can Eat added Assist Mode: change round length, change points per order, change order completion time, or turn order timeouts off. [data, GameRant, undated; Destructoid, undated] Developer calls it "the most accessible version yet".
- Two Point Hospital: designers do not want things to "diverge so rapidly" that a short break causes collapse, and also do not want returning to find millions earned. Fine line between "spoon-feedy" and "nanny-statey". [developer statement, PCGamesN]
- Cozy games: author Buergi says cozy design removes "the stress of failure", with no central threat; progress is near-effortless and idle progress continues without the player. [opinion, short commentary, In Media Res, 2024-11-12] https://mediacommons.org/imr/content/motivation-through-coziness
- Toca Boca: no goals, no time constraints, no points. [developer statement, earlier notes]
- Replacement for challenge in cozy games seen in sources: collection (Neko Atsume, Animal Restaurant journal), decoration (GPGP, Furista), variety of customers and orders (GPGP over a million order combos), and personal expression.

### Inferences (mine)
- Because Overcooked, the loudest "panic" game, felt it had to add a switch that removes order timeouts, a full timeout is a known accessibility problem.
- Two forms of removed timer: no failure at all (Toca, Animal Restaurant as read), or failure that is only a smaller reward (Sally's Spa frown, Papa's grade).

### Gaps
- No peer reviewed study on timers and 6 to 12 year olds. Three searches found nothing usable. One PMC page failed to load (bot check) and one document was unreadable.

## 8. When a queue with choice beats one customer at a time

### Takeaway
No developer says this outright. The sources imply: choice needs two or more customers waiting and different values per customer.

### Cited findings
- Order-Fulfillment paper: the genre's key decision is which waiting customer to attend. [research]
- Sally's Spa: tip versus patience differences make the choice real. [guide]
- Pokemon Cafe ReMix shows the opposite pattern: random queue where you cannot choose, and the article lists this as a grind for specific recruits. [opinion]
- Papa's and GPGP: one customer at a time, with all the skill in the task and the personality of the customer. Both are very large (Papa's top paid charts, GPGP 300M downloads). [data]
- Overcooked: designer says the basic design is "too many tasks with the amount of players you have". [developer statement]

### Inferences (mine)
- One at a time works when the job itself is rich (many steps, graded). Queue with choice works when each job is short and the interest is in ordering them.
- Hybrid seen in Furista's Cat Cafe: while customers cuddle cats, you do side tasks. Waiting time for one is work time for another.

### Gaps
- No A/B or analyst data comparing the two shapes.

## 9. Job length in the hits

Not found. No source gave seconds per customer for Diner Dash, Cooking Fever, Papa's or Sally's Spa. The only length numbers are structural: Pizza Shop Mania 48 levels; Pokemon Cafe ReMix about 2,000 levels; Overcooked early prototype about 8 levels (2017); Diner Dash sequel not stated. Papa's stations: four per shift, with orders getting more complex by the end of the day (Wikipedia). Growth pattern named in sources: more complex orders and more simultaneous demands (Overcooked: each new recipe was offset by an easier environment; Diner Dash: ramps toward dense request streams, Baggatta).

## 10. What this means for a hand-wash cat spa (my inference only)

1. Keep the choice, soften the loss. The "who next" decision is the proven core of the genre. Use customer differences (rare VIP with long job and big reward, regular quick cats, a cat that pays more but fidgets) so the best pick is not obvious. Sally's Spa tip versus patience is the model.
2. No-stress impatient customers. Do not make a customer leave and cost the player. Options in order of gentleness: (a) a waiting cat shows a cute mood (calm, then a tail flick, then a nap) and the only effect is a smaller bonus tip; (b) a waiting cat plays with a toy or flops on a cushion in the queue room and costs nothing; (c) if a cat must leave, it walks to the door, waves, and returns later in the day with a note "I will be back", never lost. This matches how Sally's Spa lets a frown cost only a heart, and how Papa's grades instead of fails. Toca and Overcooked Assist Mode support the view that a hard timeout is optional.
3. Give a free patience tool, not a paid one: a cushion, a toy, a purr-minute. Sally's Spa tea and Cooking Fever gems show the paid version is a source of complaint.
4. Helpers do one slower step. Follow Sally's Spa: a helper fills the tub or dries off a cat, while the player does the hands-on washing. Two Point's lesson: idle staff should need light attention. Avoid selling helpers with Robux speed-ups that decide who wins.
5. Customers with names, favourite shampoos and returning visits (Papa's customer files, GPGP regulars). Players complained in Papa's when favourites went missing.
6. Short first customer, one at a time, then the queue. Papa's post-mortem: untaught ticket rules made players serve the wrong customer. Introduce the second waiting cat only after the first wash is learned. This is my extension; no source gave a timing.
7. Stars grade the day, with a calm sweet spot at two stars (Two Point). Keep a spend target for coins, since Papa's found tips alone left players wanting something to buy.
8. Large touch targets and chain-friendly controls: Diner Dash's smaller targets broke chains and annoyed casual players. Earlier notes already cover kids' touch accuracy.
9. Return hooks without pressure: a permanent customer album, shop decor, new spa rooms per tier, VIP cats that visit on a schedule. Avoid limited-time events and energy meters, which draw the criticism in Pokemon Cafe ReMix.
10. Offer a relaxed setting if a timer-like element exists, in the way of Overcooked Assist Mode.
