## 1. rasdev: four buckets of how a game gets players
1. **Home recommendations** (biggest). Two steps: is the game considered, then is it right for this player (who presses play, who leaves at once, days they return, time played). No single magic number; all matter.
2. **Search and charts.** Search: only the game name really helps, so a plain name that says what you do (Chopper Forest works, Project Nova does not). Charts like top trending and up and coming matter most for new games.
3. **Ads.** Ranking on Home is separate from ads. Roblox tests, as the video quotes them: median +14% play sessions and +10% players still there a week later across 21 campaigns, not guaranteed. A weak game gets nothing lasting from ads.
4. **Outside Roblox.** TikTok is the biggest route (small accounts get 100K to 500K views). YouTube devlogs are slow; a big YouTuber playing the game is fast. A Discord builds a core of returning players. Friend invites count as an official signal (co-play days), so design for playing with friends.
5. **Returning players** are their own route: notifications, updates and events. Look at the baseline a week after each spike and keep it as high as possible.
6. **Game quality gates everything.** A weak game spikes and vanishes; a good one dips after each spike and climbs back.
Also claimed (comment, unverified): a game needs about 250 highly engaged players to show to everyone; it used to be 500 and changed in August. A commenter says they got access at 80 of 250. Not confirmed by Roblox, so do not plan on it.

What it means for Clean a Cat: matches home-recommendations.md. New or sharper points to keep: name says the action (we already do); design the co-op part (friends scrub and brush together is already in the draft); events and notifications for return; judge each update by the baseline a week later, not the spike; TikTok and Reels for cats are our cheapest outside route.

## 4. Reel by jayantcreates.ai (Oct 13): "Claude broke Instagram" (caption only)
